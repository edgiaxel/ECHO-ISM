# Game Concept — ECHO-ISM

## 1. High concept

**ECHO-ISM** is a 2D top-down time-loop puzzle game in which Greg, a small blob-like digital being, coordinates with ghostly copies of his past attempts to save a miniature digital city from collapse.

Misinformation spreads through the city. Connections fail, residents stop trusting one another, and automated systems begin to destabilize. Greg cannot repair everything in a single attempt. Instead, he records actions in short loops and uses the echoes of earlier attempts to perform several tasks in parallel.

The player does not defeat a villain through combat. The player solves practical puzzles that restore verified information, reliable connections, and cooperation before the city's systems fail.

## 2. Player fantasy

The player should feel like a clever problem-solver who can turn failed attempts into useful teammates.

The emotional progression is:

1. **Confusion:** the player encounters a puzzle and realizes one attempt is not enough.
2. **Discovery:** the player notices that recorded actions persist as echoes.
3. **Experimentation:** the player adjusts timing and action order.
4. **Coordination:** multiple versions of Greg work together.
5. **Mastery:** the player solves a challenge by planning a sequence that no single loop could complete alone.

## 3. Setting

The game takes place inside a miniature digital city: a small networked world populated by abstract blob beings and simple geometric structures. The environment resembles a hand-drawn digital illustration layered over a schematic system map.

The city was designed to let its residents communicate, create, and cooperate. Its systems are now becoming unstable because misleading claims and manipulative messages have damaged the connections between residents and disrupted shared infrastructure.

The setting is fictional and abstract. Do not associate the cause of the crisis with a real religion, ethnicity, nationality, or other protected identity.

## 4. Main character: Greg

Greg is a small, expressive blob with eyes. His silhouette should be recognizable at a glance. He can squash, stretch, wobble, blink, and leave a faint digital trail.

Greg is not a combatant or a traditional detective. He is a repairer, courier, and puzzle-solver who restores connections and verifies information.

The name Greg is intentionally simple and slightly humorous against the serious stakes. The contrast should be charming, not mocking toward people affected by misinformation or social harm.

## 5. Story premise

A wave of misleading information has begun to destabilize the miniature digital city. Residents receive incomplete or false claims, communication nodes become unreliable, and ordinary disagreements turn into increasingly harmful reactions. The city's shared systems begin to fail.

Greg discovers that the city's infrastructure can be repaired, but each repair requires actions at different locations and at different times. His echo technology records what he does during a loop. When a loop resets, the previous Greg reappears as a ghost and repeats the recorded sequence.

Greg uses this ability to investigate broken systems, verify information, reopen communication routes, and connect residents with reliable context and support. Each completed stage restores part of the city. The wider mystery can gradually reveal that the crisis is being amplified by bad actors exploiting the city's trust network, without making the player enact or optimize recruitment or extremist tactics.

## 6. Narrative principles

- Show the story through the environment, short visual events, and the consequences of puzzle actions.
- Keep dialogue short and optional where possible.
- Do not require a long narrative to understand a level's immediate objective.
- Present residents as individuals with incomplete information and different needs, not as inherently evil caricatures.
- The player should interrupt escalation, verify information, and restore connection rather than punish or shame residents.
- The antagonist may manipulate information systems, but the game should not glamorize the antagonist or provide a recruitment simulator.
- Avoid graphic violence and avoid depicting self-harm. Communicate danger through system failure, separation, warning signals, and environmental changes.
- Use fictional claims and fictional groups. Do not reproduce real-world propaganda.

## 7. Core experience pillars

### Pillar A — Past attempts become teammates
Every loop can create a useful echo. The player learns to coordinate actions across attempts.

### Pillar B — Small rules, surprising solutions
Levels are built from a small set of readable puzzle modules. Interesting outcomes emerge from their combinations.

### Pillar C — Timing matters
A door, signal, moving platform, or timing control may only be usable during a specific window. The player must plan when an echo performs an action.

### Pillar D — Prevention is active
The player takes concrete actions that stop a harmful chain of events before it completes.

### Pillar E — A readable abstract world
Simple shapes, clear silhouettes, and strong visual feedback make the game understandable without elaborate assets.

## 8. Intended first playable experience

The first playable slice should include:

- Greg moving around one compact room.
- A short loop timer.
- A way to record the attempt.
- One ghost echo that replays the previous attempt.
- One echo-compatible switch or terminal.
- One second objective the player can complete while the echo acts.
- A door that opens only when both tasks are coordinated.
- A clear success state and a way to retry.

The player should be able to understand the basic mechanic without reading this document.

## 9. Narrative progression across stages

The exact number of story stages is not fixed yet. Use a small set of reusable level templates, with each stage introducing or combining a mechanic.

Suggested thematic progression:

1. **Boot Sequence:** discover movement, loops, and echoes.
2. **Broken Route:** use an echo to hold or activate a mechanism while Greg takes another path.
3. **Signal Integrity:** verify a message before it reaches another node.
4. **Timing Window:** coordinate an echo with a moving or timed mechanism.
5. **Split Protocol:** solve a puzzle that requires several echoes to operate separate mechanisms.
6. **Network Recovery:** combine multiple modules to reconnect isolated residents and restore a section of the city.
7. **System Stabilization:** final challenge using previously learned mechanics; no new major mechanic is required.

These are thematic chapter labels, not a commitment to seven fully bespoke levels. Each chapter can use multiple variations of a shared template.

## 10. Success criteria

The concept is working when:

- The echo mechanic is necessary to solve at least one puzzle.
- The player understands that each echo replays a past attempt.
- Multiple echoes can perform different tasks at the same time.
- The player can predict and adjust the timing of recorded actions.
- A failed attempt gives useful information for the next attempt.
- The theme is conveyed through restoring connections and verifying information, not just through text.
